The problem

What if a window could generate electricity?

Glass lets daylight in and gives us a view. Regular windows do not make electricity. Roof solar panels do, but they are not see-through.

01 / Sunlight enters

The window stays clear.

Sunlight reaches the glass. Most of the window stays clear, with no solar cells across the middle.

02 / Catch some light

A little invisible light is caught.

A thin layer may take in some ultraviolet (UV) light, which our eyes cannot see. Most visible sunlight can still pass through.

03 / The layer responds

The thin layer takes in light.

The material is meant to respond to the light it catches. It still needs to be made and tested.

04 / Give off light

The layer gives off new light.

The material may give off light inside the glass. It is still light here, not electricity.

05 / Move through the glass

Light bounces toward the edges.

Some of the light may bounce inside the glass and travel to an edge. Some light will be lost along the way.

06 / Reach the edge

The light moves to the frame.

Small solar cells sit around the window’s edge, ready to receive light that reaches them.

07 / Solar cells receive light

The edge cells take in the light.

The clear middle remains free of solar cells. The cells are only around the edge.

08 / Make electricity

Now the light becomes power.

The solar cells turn the light into electricity. This is the first point where electricity appears.

09 / Send power out

Wires carry power away.

Wires could carry the power to a meter, a small device, a building or a battery.

This is a design idea. It has not been tested yet.

How the material might be made

From farm waste
to a window layer.

We are exploring whether farm waste could help make tiny carbon-based materials called Carbon Quantum Dots (CQDs). Each step is an idea to test, not a finished process.

Long-term goal: make a layer that could last 20–25 years in a building. This is a target, not a result.

01 / Start with farm waste

Some farm leftovers contain carbon.

Things like citrus peel could be explored. We have not chosen or tested a source yet.

02 / Prepare it

Clean and prepare the material.

The waste may need to be cleaned and prepared first. The exact method has not been chosen.

03 / Make carbon material

A step toward tiny carbon particles.

Preparation may make a carbon-rich material. What it contains would need to be tested.

04 / Make tiny dots

Study Carbon Quantum Dots.

These tiny carbon-based materials are called Carbon Quantum Dots, or CQDs. We still need to find out how to make them and how they handle light.

05 / Clean and test

Separate the material and study it.

The particles would need to be cleaned and tested. No method or result is claimed.

An earlier idea raised a key question: can the material last in strong sunlight?

06 / Mix with clear material

Can it stay clear?

The tiny particles might be mixed into a clear material. We would need to test whether it stays see-through and does not scatter too much light.

07 / Make a thin layer

A layer that catches and gives off light.

The goal is a thin layer that catches some sunlight and gives off light inside the glass. It has not been made yet.

08 / Fit it into the window

Place the layer inside clear glass.

The layer would be one part of the window. Small solar cells at the edge could turn its guided light into electricity.

More about the idea

Curious how it works?

Open a topic for a little more detail. The main idea stays simple: catch light, guide it to the edge, and let solar cells make power.

How does the window catch light?+

A thin layer may catch some sunlight and give off light inside the glass. This is the main idea being explored.

How does light reach the edge?+

Some light may bounce inside the glass until it reaches the edge. Scientists call this “total internal reflection.” The glass and its surfaces affect how well it works.

What are Carbon Quantum Dots?+

They are tiny materials made mostly from carbon. Researchers study them because some kinds can give off light. The type and properties for AETHER have not been chosen or tested.

Why might some light be lost?+

Some light may escape, get scattered or be caught again by the material. Sunlight, water and heat may also change the layer over time.

What would the first test check?+

A small test would check each step: light entering the window, light reaching its edge, and electricity coming from the solar cells.
